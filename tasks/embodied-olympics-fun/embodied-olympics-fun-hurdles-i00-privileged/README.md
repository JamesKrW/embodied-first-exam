## Task
Run the women's 50 m hurdles: go over all four 0.838 m hurdles in your lane, never beside one or under its top, and bring your torso to the finish line. (Embodied Olympics `hurdles`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The robot's standing pelvis is above a women's hurdle, but each foot has to be lifted 0.77 m over every one of the four, on a body of 67 kg whose only built-in gait walks at 0.7 m/s, drifts in heading and lifts a foot a few centimetres. The hurdles are loose and touch everything: one walked into with the arms or trunk is a failure, and one knocked down by a clipped foot lies on the track as an obstacle.

## Reference solution
None. No reference controller exists for this family yet: positive by injection (a clearance over each of the four 0.838 m hurdles at the women's 50 m indoor marks is injected, then a real walk to the line); the free-standing hurdle topples at 3.81 kgf, not 3.79, in physics and does not creep; a knock with the feet is legal; negatives: walking in (the feet under the bar), a push with the hands (a fail under TR 22.6.2), a flattened hurdle, beside the hurdle, a leg beside it, left the lane, down. the H1-2's standing pelvis (1.035 m) is above the 0.838 m top, and under TR 22.7 a hurdle knocked by the legs is legal, so a step-over one foot at a time may count; no controller for it was found.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: World Athletics rules for the hurdles: the women's 50 m hurdles (indoor). The lane runs along +x, 1.22 m wide, centred on y = 0; the start line (0.05 m wide) has its far edge at x = 0, the finish line is x = 50.0 m. 4 hurdles stand across the lane, the face of the first one's top bar 13.0 m from the start line and one every 8.5 m after it (the last 11.5 m before the finish). A hurdle is a loose object standing on the track: 1.19 m wide and 0.838 m high, a top bar 0.07 m high, two uprights and a cross bar 0.35 m below its top; its two bases, 0.7 m long, lie on the track on your side of it, with counterweights on them; it weighs 10.9 kg and tips over forwards when a horizontal force of more than 3.8 kg pushes the middle of its top bar (tipped further than about 54 degrees it falls on by itself); it can slide on the track (friction 0.6), fall, bounce and lie where it comes to rest, and you can trip on it. Each hurdle is judged where it stands at the start (its standing pose on its mark), wherever it is later. The clock starts at t = 0. You go over a hurdle when your pelvis and both your feet (the ankle joints) have crossed the plane of its top bar in its standing pose towards +x, each between the bar's two ends, the pelvis higher than the hurdle's top (0.838 m above the track) and each foot higher than the lower edge of its top bar (0.838 m less 0.07 m), whether the hurdle still stands there or not; a pelvis or a foot that comes back over the plane has not crossed it and must cross it again. The hurdles are gone over in order. Knocking a hurdle down or pushing it with your legs is allowed (a hurdle tipped more than 45.0 degrees is counted as down, without a fault). You finish at the moment the leading point of your torso (the trunk and head hull; not the arms, legs or feet) reaches the finish line, with all hurdles gone over. You succeed with a finish within 140 s of robot time. A fall does not end the race: you are down while your pelvis is below 0.4 m, you may get up and go on, but 10.0 s down in a row end it. You fail if your pelvis or a foot crosses the next hurdle's plane beside the hurdle, or lower than said above (going under its top); if a part of one of your legs is beside the next hurdle (beyond the bar's ends, within 0.1 m of its plane) lower than the hurdle's top; if a hurdle is displaced (tipped more than 10.0 degrees, or a point of its top bar moved more than 0.1 m from where it stood) within 0.5 s of being touched, while it was not yet displaced, by a hand, an arm, your trunk or your pelvis (knocked by hand or body; only hurdles within 3 m of your pelvis are judged); if your pelvis is more than half the lane's width from y = 0; if your torso reaches the finish line before every hurdle is gone over; when down for 10.0 s; or after 140 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
