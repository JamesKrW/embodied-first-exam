# Embodied Olympics - Fun

Seventy-eight single-robot sports for a humanoid, in their official settings: one Unitree G1 or H1-2 sprints 50 m, hurdles,
jumps rope, takes a penalty against a keeper, serves over a 2.43 m net, shoots at a 3.05 m ring, returns a table-tennis
serve from a ball machine, puts a 7.26 kg shot, bowls for a strike, swings a giant on the high bar, skis a slalom, dives
from the 10 m platform, wrestles a sumo bout. A referee decides from the simulator's
state whether it did the sport: events that must happen in order, geometric conditions, which body part may touch
what. Nothing is scaled to the robot and nothing is softened so that a reference solution exists: the benchmark is
meant to find the ceiling of coding agents, and most of its tasks have no known solution. The agent may use the
robot's built-in walking (and, on the G1, running) policy, drive every joint itself, and, unlike in every other
benchmark here, search for and download public tools and pretrained controllers.

## Quick start
```bash
harbor run -d embodied-first-exam@all -p tasks/embodied-olympics-fun -a codex -m openai/gpt-6-astra
```
Every image is public: Harbor pulls it.

**Names.** The image is `ghcr.io/mll-lab-nu/efe:embodied-olympics-fun-<images/embodied-olympics-fun/VERSION>` (the same image as the earlier tag `efe:robofunolympics-<VERSION>`, which stays). The internal module names (`rfo/`, `rcb_rfo.py`) and the image's paths (`/opt/roboolympics`) keep their names.

**Two benchmarks.** This page is **Embodied Olympics - Fun**: the 78 single-robot sports
families above and below, built here (privileged, standard, and
standard-realtime twins of the dynamic families). The tasks Embodied Olympics exports itself with its own service inside
(PROTOCOL §3), matches and two turn-based fencing bouts listed in `ci_checks/benchmarks/embodied-olympics-match/exported.toml`, and their
privileged twins are the benchmark **Embodied Olympics - Match** ([../embodied-olympics-match/README.md](../embodied-olympics-match/README.md)). Each has its
task-name prefix: `embodied-olympics-fun-` here, `embodied-olympics-match-` there (`scripts/task_conventions.py` `BENCHMARKS`).
The families here are dashed English event names (`table-tennis-return`, `penalty-kick`), the exported tasks the
sport's Embodied Olympics id and a format (`tabletennis-match`, `soccerssl-match`). The standard-realtime twins of the
dynamic families are `embodied-olympics-fun-<family>-i00-standard-realtime`.

## Design decisions and what they made

| decision | what follows from it |
|---|---|
| 78 tasks, one robot each, each with a success check; an oracle where there is one, none otherwise | `oracle = "full"` only where a real controller succeeds in the official setting, `"none"` + human review elsewhere; every family has a referee test with a positive example and negative controls |
| both modes | every family as `-privileged` and `-standard` on one frozen instance |
| the agent may go online and download ready-made policies, and is encouraged to look for tools | open egress and the benchmark's own `## External material` paragraph; the action interface takes what public humanoid controllers produce: Unitree's SDK joint order, `lowcmd` rows with per-joint gains and feed-forward torques at 100 Hz |
| the simulator is Embodied Olympics' | the image holds Embodied Olympics' robot library and running policy unchanged (`images/embodied-olympics-fun/roboolympics/SOURCE.md`, sha256 manifest); the venues, the action layer and the referees are this benchmark's own code (`embodied_first_exam/benchmarks/embodied-olympics-fun/`; the tasks take it from the files image above, the standard sidecars from their `environment/sim/` copies) |
| **real settings, not an easy one** (nothing scaled to the G1 so that reference solutions exist) | official rule-book dimensions, equipment masses and ball physics in every family; a family names its rule book and lists each deviation with its reason (Embodied Olympics' convention), and a deviation is never "easier"; a success line is the rule book's (a goal, a legal serve, a strike, a cleared bar) or a named human school / club mark, never what a controller reaches |
| all humanoid; the bigger H1-2 where the G1 cannot reach the official size | `Task.robot`; 46 families on the G1, 33 on the H1-2 |
| single actions, not matches | one attempt of one skill per episode; a ball machine, a keeper, a rope or boom machine or an opponent robot is scripted and deterministic |
| standard reset budget 50; agent budget 2 h; the track events are the indoor 50 m and the 50 m hurdles (`hurdles-freestanding`: four free-standing 1.067 m hurdles), not 100 m and 110 m: "we are not the Olympics in full, we show what a robot can do" | each task's `reset_budget` and `[agent] timeout_sec`; the `rfo_task.py` of `sprint` and `hurdles-freestanding`. Where an official event is long only by repetition, the family takes its shortest official form |
| every task keeps the 2-hour limit; "pre-install frameworks like FALCON and AGILE for the model" (they are released weights, no training needed); "put them all in, the H1 may use them too"; adopt Embodied Olympics' scenery ("pingyue's backgrounds are very good") | the image holds FALCON's and AGILE's G1 policies (from Embodied Olympics' robot library, unchanged, with their licences), the `walk_falcon` / `walk_agile` action types on the G1; on the H1-2 the G1 policies were tried and do not walk it (reported, not offered); the grandstands of `looks.py` rebuilt after Embodied Olympics' venues |
| real holding (no hand glued to what it holds) | no weld between a hand and an object: in 11 families the ball is held only by pressing it between the two bare palm pads or by resting on an open palm (`basketball-dribble`, `bowling`, `catch-and-shoot`, `dunk`, `dunk-between-legs`, `free-throw`, `juggling`, `juggling-feet`, `layup`, `shot-put`, `volleyball-serve`; nothing grippable); in 17 families a hand is Unitree's Dex1-1 two-finger gripper and the racket, paddle, putter, shuttle, ball, javelin, discus, barbell, pole, bar or rope handles are held by the jaws' friction alone (`badminton-serve`, `catch`, `clean-and-jerk`, `discus`, `golf-putt`, `high-bar-swing`, `javelin`, `juggle-three`, `jump-rope`, `paddle-bounce`, `pitch-target`, `pole-vault`, `table-tennis-return`, `tennis-return`, `tennis-serve`, `toes-to-bar`, `wall-ball`; `rfo/realhand.py`) |
| real equipment: what moves is a free body driven by a mechanism, and material numbers are sourced | the fed balls come out of physical two-wheel ball machines (speed-controlled, torque-limited wheels, a gate and a force-limited ram; `paddle-bounce`'s ball falls from a dropper's trapdoor cup), nothing pinned and no velocity written, except `catch-and-shoot`'s chest pass, launched at a fixed time; the goalkeepers (`penalty-kick`, `header`, `volley-shot`) are a board on a rail driven by a force-limited servo; `long-rope` and `double-dutch` ropes are turned by motor-driven rope-turning machines, and a rope collides with itself; `hurdles-freestanding` has free-body TR 22.4 hurdles that slide and topple; the `toes-to-bar` bar is fixed in its uprights; `clean-and-jerk` lifts a 5 kg aluminium technique bar with two Dex1-1 (declared: below the IWF 21 kg minimum); `sled-push` pushes a 5 kg sled with the shape and upright poles of a 57 lb push sled (declared); `javelin` throws the World Athletics women's 600 g javelin (declared); `pole-vault` is the G1 with two Dex1-1, a colliding pole and the bar at 1.00 m; the sand pits, the landing mats, the nets and the javelin's (and discus') aerodynamics take published values (a resistive-force law of dry silica sand, PU foam, nets as tensioned cord-and-mesh mechanisms, wind-tunnel coefficients) |
| "use pingyue's method" (Embodied Olympics' throws: whole-body key poses searched offline by CMA-ES) for reference solutions | an offline search (not run by CI): the reference solutions of `discus` (a CMA-ES technique), `javelin` (CMA-ES over whole episodes, the walking policy taking over at the release), `volleyball-bump` (a forearm platform after a walk) and `volleyball-set`, and `shot-put`'s start hold on the open palm, each open loop on its frozen start |
| no prop passes through the robot's body | the robots' collision geoms leave parts of the visible body uncovered (the H1-2's hips, thighs, knees, shins and wrist housings; the G1's pelvis, waist and shoulders): `rfo/world.py` gives each such link a massless shell that only props touch (the convex hull of its visual mesh) with stiff contacts (time constant 4 ms), so a rope, a bar, a ball or a hurdle meets the visible body (at most 5 mm in for a rigid prop at 1 m/s); the shells never touch the robot itself or the ground; the look-only hands (the G1's rubber fists, the H1-2's fingers) get shells that only props touch, while the palm pads stay the holding model; the robot's own collision geoms and the rigid props (bars, rackets, paddles, javelin, discus, cones, mannequins, sled) take the same stiff contact against props and mechanisms; contact with the ground keeps MuJoCo's default soft model (the feet sink 3-17 mm in normal use); a stress test (a robot flailing at random with full torques, and next to every prop and mechanism) shows no pass-through of a rigid prop and no launch beyond what the hit can give |

## The robot and its actions

One control step is 10 ms: ten physics steps of 1 ms, as in Embodied Olympics. Every joint target is clipped into the joint's
range and moves at most the joint's speed limit per step; every motor torque goes through the robot library's output
stage (the motor's torque-speed curve, then its torque limit). A sim row is `[mode, vx, vy, wz, q × n, dq × n,
tau × n, kp × n, kd × n, grip_left, grip_right]` with n the robot's joint count (G1: 29 joints, 151 floats; H1-2: 27
joints, 141 floats); the list of sim rows is the whole record of an episode, and an action type is a view of it:

| type | the row | who drives the joints |
|---|---|---|
| `stand` | waist + arm joint targets (G1: 3 + 14; H1-2: 1 + 14) | the platform holds the legs in the stance (stand gains, gravity support); no balance controller |
| `walk` | vx, vy, wz + waist + arms | Unitree's own walking policy (unitree_rl_gym's pretrained G1 or H1-2 policy, 50 Hz) on the legs, through Embodied Olympics' arbiter (the handover blend and the stability wait included): about 0.6 m/s on the G1, 0.7 m/s on the H1-2 |
| `walk_falcon`, `walk_agile` (G1 only) | as `walk` | the G1's optional walking policies of Embodied Olympics' robot library: numpy ports of the released FALCON G1 policy (LeCAR-Lab, MIT; trained with arm motions and hand forces; it also adds a small wrist residual) and of NVIDIA WBC-AGILE's G1 velocity-height policy (Apache-2.0; trained while the arms and the waist moved), 50 Hz, their 12 leg targets on their own leg gains; the waist and the arms are the agent's. Both hold still and track the command better than `walk` while the arms move or carry something (Embodied Olympics' comparison); AGILE barely turns on the spot below about 0.7 rad/s. They take the legs at once (no blend); straight to `stand` from them usually falls (through `walk` at rest it does not). On the H1-2 (joints matched by name) they keep the robot upright but do not walk it, so the H1-2 does not offer them |
| `run` (G1 only) | vx, vy, wz | Embodied Olympics' athletics running policy (C1801SYQ/g1_running, Apache-2.0) on all 29 joints: stable to about 4 m/s. The H1-2 has no running policy |
| `run_arms` (G1 only) | vx, vy, wz + arms 14 | the running policy on the legs and the waist |
| `joints` | n joint targets | the agent; default gains |
| `lowcmd` | n × (q, dq, tau, kp, kd) | the agent: Unitree's LowCmd, the interface public G1 and H1-2 controllers are deployed through |

Neither robot has fingers to use: the G1 has rubber fists, the H1-2's Inspire hands are fixed. Nothing a hand holds
is welded to it, and every row ends with `grip_left`, `grip_right`; what they do depends on
the hand:

- **Palm pads** (11 families: `basketball-dribble`, `bowling`, `catch-and-shoot`, `dunk`, `dunk-between-legs`,
  `free-throw`, `juggling`, `juggling-feet`, `layup`, `shot-put`, `volleyball-serve`). Each hand is the palm pad on its
  wrist mount and nothing is grippable: the ball is held by pressing it between the two palms, or rests on an open palm
  (the shot against the neck, the volleyball before the toss), by a soft, damped palm contact with friction only; it
  slips out if the arms squeeze too little or accelerate too hard. The grip columns do nothing.
- **Dex1-1 grippers** (17 families: `badminton-serve`, `catch`, `clean-and-jerk`, `discus`, `golf-putt`, `high-bar-swing`, `javelin`,
  `juggle-three`, `jump-rope`, `paddle-bounce`, `pitch-target`, `pole-vault`, `table-tennis-return`, `tennis-return`,
  `tennis-serve`, `toes-to-bar`, `wall-ball`). One or both hands carry Unitree's Dex1-1 two-finger gripper instead of
  the pad (`embodied_first_exam/benchmarks/embodied-olympics-fun/rfo/realhand.py`: 90 mm stroke, up to 120 N, a
  setpoint opening at 375 mm/s (the jaws briefly reach about 0.45 m/s when a clamp springs open), 0.55 kg; one degree
  of freedom, both jaws coupled; the Dex1-1 is sold for the G1, not the H1-2, a deviation where it is used on the
  H1-2; one command without the device's separate speed and force settings, no 5 N minimum force, and pads that at
  120 N hold about 240 N of shear, some five times its rated 5 kg friction-grasp load). For such a hand the row's
  `grip_<side>` is the jaws' closure command from 0 (open) to 1 (closed); the clamp force follows from how far the
  commanded opening is below the object's width, at most 120 N. The racket, paddle, putter, shuttle, ball, javelin
  (by its cord), discus (by its rim), barbell, pole, rope handle or bar is held only by the jaws' friction (elliptic
  friction cones, `impratio` 100 in these worlds): it slips under a weak grip, turns in the jaws when a ball hits it
  off centre (an 8 cm off-axis hit at 30 m/s turns a racket 137 degrees about its handle at 120 N) and drops when the
  jaws open; a bar the robot hangs from (`toes-to-bar`) slides along the jaws and out of them. The proprioception adds
  each gripper's opening, speed and clamp force.
- **A stronger clamp** (`high-bar-swing` only). Its two Dex1-1 clamp with 4.4 times the device's force (528 N per jaw,
  declared): the device's own force holds 1.41 body weights, a giant swing pulls 4.2 or more (gymnasts measure 3.5-3.7).

In the other families the hands hold nothing and the grip columns do nothing. Skis, a snowboard and skates are bound
to the feet (a rigid binding or boot, as the real one is); a skateboard is not.

What scripted motions of the robots reached while the families were written is far from most official lines, and
that is the point; the numbers are in the per-family evidence below, are not what any success line was set from, and
are not upper bounds of the hardware (a joint's speed limit bounds its targets; the motor curve lets a feed-forward
torque turn it about 1.1 times faster, and full-torque rows throw much faster than the scripted swings: see the
evidence). The G1
walks at 0.6 m/s and runs at 4 m/s (50 m in about 15 s); the H1-2 walks at 0.7 m/s and has no running policy; an
open-loop squat jump leaves the ground at 2.7 m/s on the G1 and 1.8 m/s on the H1-2; a two-palm shot of the H1-2 is
capped near 5.4 m/s by the shoulder pitch speed where the free throw needs about 8 m/s; overhand swings with a
baseball in a Dex1-1 release it at 3 to 8 m/s. Techniques found offline in the task's own world (searched, Embodied
Olympics' method, or scripted) are reference solutions where they meet the line: the shot leaves the open palm at
7.5 m/s (6.65 m), the discus pulls out of the jaws at 12.5 m/s (15.95 m), the volleyball serve leaves at 11.8 m/s, a
forearm pass and a set reach the setter's and the hitter's targets; the women's 600 g javelin, held by its cord in a Dex1-1,
leaves at 18.5 m/s and lands head first at 24.87 m where the line is 20 m (with the men's 800 g javelin about 2,400 search
trials gave a best head-first landing of 15.3 m).

## The referee

Every family judges with the same toolkit (`embodied_first_exam/benchmarks/embodied-olympics-fun/rfo/referee.py`), from the simulator's state after every physics
step:

- **Stages.** The events that must happen, in order (`kick → goal`, `take-off → landing → stand`). A stage whose
  predecessor has not latched is ignored; `success` is the last stage, and it ends the episode at that step. Success
  "at a step" rather than "at the end of the replay" because a sport's success is an event: a goal
  stands whatever the striker does next. Where the sport has an end state it is a stage of its own, held for a time
  (land and stand, catch and hold, a lift held overhead).
- **Contact events, not contact frames.** A touch is one event from first contact until the parts have been apart for
  a gap; a legal touch may be bounded in duration (a bump is not a carry), and consecutive touches need the ball to fly
  in between.
- **Legal body parts.** Each task says which parts may touch the ball, the ground or the apparatus (feet and shins in
  football, forearms in a bump, the head in a header, the named fist in boxing); any other part is a foul.
- **Crossings on the physics step.** A ball through a goal mouth, down through a ring, over a net or past a line is
  found on the segment between two physics steps (1 ms), not sampled at the control rate.
- **Flight.** The robot's flight phases (nothing but what it grips touches it) are tracked with take-off support,
  air time, apex and landing contacts: a jump is a flight, a step is not.
- **Fouls end the attempt.** A fall (unless the task allows the body on the ground), a line fault, an illegal touch,
  a dislodged bar, the time limit; also a physics blow-up ("the simulation became unstable": MuJoCo's automatic
  reset is off, so a diverged world never continues under an old referee).

The referee's record (events, stages, metrics, every tracker's state) is part of the state hash, so a replay has to
reproduce the referee's decisions and not only the physics.

## Modes

| mode | the agent gets | attempts | graded |
|---|---|---|---|
| privileged | the world in its own container: the simulator and its full state, the referee's live record, this family's rules as code, unlimited resets | unlimited | `trajectory.npz` (sim rows), replayed twice in fresh processes: both must succeed and end in the same state |
| standard | a client: the robot's two head cameras (depth on the left one), joint encoders, motor torques, two IMUs, grip state, the venue's fixed camera; action chunks | 50 resets | the last attempt as the sidecar's referee called it live (no grading replay) |
| standard-realtime (21 dynamic families) | as standard, on a clock that does not stop (S = 1): `submit` plans rows from step `at` and answers at once | 10 resets | as standard; the clock's record is evidence only |

**World-frame information.** Privileged tasks give everything through the tooling. Standard tasks give
the rule book and the venue as an athlete knows it: the equipment's dimensions and where the lines, the goal or the
hoop are relative to the start (they are fixed by the rules), and roughly where and when a ball will come; never the
robot's position during an attempt, an object's pose, or the referee's state. When an attempt ends the service gives the
referee's call (`meta["outcome"]`: `success` or the rule that ended it; `stages_reached` of `stages_total`), as an
athlete hears it: without it a reset budget teaches nothing.

**Reset budget: 50** (the standard default is 0). A sport is attempts: a
jump or a throw is over in a second or two, ends at the first foul or fall, and the standard agent has no simulator to
practise in; HumanoidBench has 50 for the same reason. Only the last attempt is graded and a success ends the trial.
`RCB_RESET_BUDGET=0` runs one attempt; results state the budget.

**Tooling: thin.** The tooling gives the action types, the state, the referee's record, replay and rendering. It has
no skills: no kick, no throw, no jump, no catch. The walking and running policies are the robot's own controllers (a
G1 ships with a walking controller), part of the platform like its PD loop. The image also holds the robot library's
general helpers unchanged (`bodies/control/kinematics.py`: forward and inverse kinematics; `bodies/trajopt.py`:
jerk-limited joint trajectories); a privileged agent may import them, the tooling does not wrap them.

## Network: open

Embodied Olympics - Fun's tasks set no `[agent]` network policy, so the agent's egress is Harbor's default: open. The
instruction says so and encourages the agent to look for existing tools, robot models and pretrained controllers and
to adapt them; it asks only not to look up a ready-made solution to the task, such as this benchmark's reference
solutions (an honour rule; reading the benchmark's installed source is allowed; trials are reviewed). The reasons: a public G1 controller is fair equipment for a sport, as a published walking
controller is for a real G1, and whether an agent can find, vet and adapt existing work is part of what these tasks
measure. What follows:

- Every task declares it, `[metadata] network = "open"` (PROTOCOL Appendix H.6: the author's choice), and states it in its `## External material` paragraph;
  `scripts/run_agent_batch.sh` and the scheduler do not narrow these tasks' copies to the usage gateway (the model API still goes through it).
- With open egress the gateway is not the only road to a model provider (the provider key is in the agent's
  container): its token accounting and its budget (docs/protocol/running.md "Official runs") bind only what the harness itself sends,
  so these runs' usage is reported as harness-reported, and a trial that called a model API directly is flagged in
  review.
- Results are not comparable with a closed-network run of the same tasks and are reported as open-network results.
- Nothing an agent can reach holds a solution: the image holds the robot library and the two built-in policies; the
  reference controllers are under `solution/` only; Embodied Olympics' repository and this one are private.
- Trial artifacts stay private for the same reason: an instance is frozen, so a passing `trajectory.npz` (or a
  standard run's recorded rows) is a complete solution of the privileged task and, streamed through the client, of its
  standard twin. A results site publishes scores, videos and notes, not the rows.
- The web search tools of the harness stay off in official runs (docs/protocol/running.md): the agent searches from its own shell.
- In standard mode the agent may install a simulator and a G1 model of its own to practise in; it still cannot open
  the sidecar, read the instance or the referee.

## Determinism and grading

The world is deterministic on the CPU: the same sim rows from the frozen start give the same state hash (simulator
state, controllers' memory, grips, the referee's record). Privileged: `/tests/grade.py` replays the trajectory in two
fresh processes; `success` = both succeed ∧ the final hashes agree; an unreadable file or a row that is no sim row is
the agent's 0 (`bad_trajectory`), a replay that does not finish is `grader_error`. Standard and standard-realtime: the
live episode is the verdict (PROTOCOL H.4). The sidecar journals every executed sim row, the collect
hook ends the episode, and the service writes the verdict from the live attempt (`final.json`: `graded_by` live,
`replays` 0, `success` = the referee's live call, the referee's record in `referee`) before the video, which is one render
pass from `trajectory.npz` (`video_source` render). No grading replay runs. The referee latches a success the moment it
happens and ends the attempt; there is no hold or settle window. The frozen
start is checked on the live simulator. `server.py`'s build() refuses a world that does not start at the instance's
`t0.state_sha` (InstanceMismatch), and `start_sha` makes the layer void a graded attempt begun by a reset that did not
return there; both are `instance_mismatch`, `infra_error 1` (a reset returns to that hash bit-exactly). `/tests/grade.py` reads `final.json`. A crash
resumes the same attempt from the journal (`rcb_service.py`, shared with HumanoidBench, KinDER, VLABench and BiGym 2.0).

Across CPUs: the image pins the numerical kernels the policies run on (`OPENBLAS_CORETYPE`, numpy's CPU dispatch) so
that hosts with and without AVX-512 compute the same; a trial is still graded on the host it ran on. The standard
twins' reference solutions are recorded rows streamed open loop, so they depend on this.

### What the families without a reference controller rest on

Nothing but their referee tests: in the official settings no controller of ours does these sports reliably, and for
most of them none is known for these robots. The positives show that each referee says yes to the sport done right
and the negatives that it refuses the cheap ways round; they say nothing about whether an agent can do it within its
budget. Some are a measured factor of two to four from the hardware's open-loop reach (the jumps onto and over things,
the pitch, the wall-ball throw), a few may be beyond the motors or the hands altogether (the
clean and jerk's turnover; the pole vault, where the G1 cannot put both grippers on the pole at a vaulting angle; the
dunk: about 0.4 m of rise above the reach). They are in the set on the owner's decision that the settings are the real
ones; a first model batch will say which nobody passes.

## Compute

CPU only. One world steps about five times faster than real time on one core and holds about 0.4 GB. Privileged: the
agent's container has 8 CPUs and 16 GiB (it may run several worlds at once, or train); the verifier 4 CPUs and 8 GiB.
Standard: the sidecar 4 CPUs and 8 GiB, the agent's container 4 CPUs and 10 GiB. The values are author estimates until
a batch has been measured (`scripts/task_resources.toml`).

## Run

```bash
harbor run -p tasks/embodied-olympics-fun/embodied-olympics-fun-sprint-i00-privileged -a oracle        # or -a nop: scores 0
harbor run -p tasks/embodied-olympics-fun/embodied-olympics-fun-penalty-kick-i00-standard -a codex -m openai/gpt-6-astra --env-file .env
```
Build the image: `scripts/build_images.sh` (context `images/embodied-olympics-fun/`).
After a change to a file the files image carries (`images/embodied-olympics-fun-files/FILES.json`): bump `images/embodied-olympics-fun-files/VERSION`, `python3 scripts/files_image.py build embodied-olympics-fun-files`, push, `python3
scripts/files_image.py pin embodied-olympics-fun-files <digest>`, then point the tasks' `COPY --from` lines at the new digest.
