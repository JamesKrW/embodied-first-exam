## Task
Return the serve: after the tennis ball has bounced once in your service box, hit it with the racket held in your right hand's gripper back over the net so that it lands in the singles court on the far side, without letting go of the racket. (Embodied Olympics `tennis-return`: one Unitree H1-2 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
The ball crosses the whole court in about a second and is still moving at more than 15 m/s when it reaches you, so the racket has to be in a path a few centimetres wide at an instant you must predict, and a robot that only walks cannot go far to meet it. The racket is held only by a two-finger gripper's friction: a hard swing and the ball's impact twist it in the grip, so the face the ball meets is not where the arm put it, and a swing strong enough to return the ball over the net must not fling the racket out of the hand.

## Reference solution
`solution/oracle.py` drives the robot through the task's own tooling, closed loop on the privileged state, and writes the trajectory (`solution/solve.sh`). Reference: walks 0.7 m, then a backhand with the free racket in the right Dex1-1, the swing at 3.99 s timed for the machine's serve; the two-wheel ball machine serves at 30.3 m/s, bouncing 2 cm from its target point, and the return lands 2.3 m past the net; 10 of 10 starts moved by up to 4 cm succeed. Negatives: two bounces, dropped, thrown, the racket out of the hand in the ball's path, the gripper, the forearm, a volley, a double hit, a carry, short, long, wide, into the net, through the net, line in and out, the racket on the post, the robot in the net, the far side, touched before the serve, a fall. Measured (make_oracles.py): success after 5.93 s of the 10 s limit, live and in a fresh replay, ending in the same state.

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: The court is the ITF singles court: 23.77 m long and 8.23 m wide, measured to the outside of the lines, with a service line 6.4 m from the net on each side. The net stands across the middle, 0.914 m high at its centre and rising in a straight line to 1.07 m at the posts, which stand 0.914 m outside the sidelines. The ball is the ITF ball (radius 0.0335 m, 0.0577 kg, with air drag and spin lift). The racket is a separate body, 0.685 m long and 0.3 kg, balanced 0.33 m from the butt, with a strung surface 0.33 m by 0.25 m and an octagonal handle 0.0335 m across its flats and 0.19 m long; at the start its handle is closed in your right hand's two-finger gripper, which holds it by friction only: it can slip, turn in the gripper and fall. The racket is in your hand while its handle touches both of the gripper's jaws; it has left your hand when for more than 0.05 s the handle has not touched both jaws. About 3.0 s after the start a ball machine behind the far baseline serves the ball at 30.0 m/s into your right service box; it is served when it has left the machine's wheels. In order: the ball bounces once on your side; the racket, in your hand, hits it (the strings, the frame or any other part of the racket counts); its centre passes the net's plane from your side to the far side, above the net's top edge or outside a post (it may touch the net or a post on the way); and its first contact with the ground after that is inside the far half of the singles court, the lines included (the point under the ball's centre decides; ground contacts less than 0.02 s apart are one bounce): that is the success. You fail if the racket leaves your hand at any time (dropped, thrown or slipped out of the gripper), if the racket touches the ball before its bounce (a volley), if the ball bounces a second time on your side before your hit, if after your hit the ball touches the ground on your side or outside the far court, if the ball touches any part of you (the gripper included) at any time after the serve, if the racket touches the ball a second time (contacts less than 0.05 s apart are one hit) or stays on it longer than 0.05 s, if the ball passes the net's plane between the posts below the net's top edge, if any part of you or the racket touches the net or a post, or the ground beyond the net's plane, if you or the racket touch the ball before it is served, if you fall, or after 10 s. A fall is: your pelvis lower than 0.35 m above the ground, or any part of you other than your feet touching the ground; the racket may touch the ground on your side as long as it stays in your hand.
