## Task
Standing on the block under the high bar, jump up and catch the bar with both grippers, then do one giant swing: take your extended body in a full circle around the bar, without letting go. (Embodied Olympics `high-bar-swing`: one Unitree G1 on the Embodied Olympics simulator, judged by a referee of events, geometry and contact rules.)

## Difficulty
A real jump: the legs must lift the whole robot about 0.15 m so that the bar enters the open jaws, and the jaws must close in flight. Then nothing pushes you: the swing has to be pumped up from a hang by the legs, the waist and the shoulders, against motors of 88 N m at the hips and 25 N m in the arms, until the body goes over the top of the bar extended; the bottom of a giant swing pulls more than four times your weight through the grippers and the arms, and the grippers slide round the fixed bar against their own friction.

## Reference solution
None. The clamp force: the device's 120 N holds 1.41 body weights with both hands, a giant swing pulls at least 4.2 (gymnasts 3.5-3.7); 240, 360, 420 and 480 N per jaw all lose the bar in the same magic-action swing, 528 N completes it. Negatives: nop (time), no catch (jaws open: back on the block, time), starting already hanging (held the bar without jumping to it), grippers opened (let go), a fall from the block (the mats), a tucked circle and a circle that turns back (referee-only rotations: no count); stress: 4 s of random joint targets on the bar held (2.9 body weights), then the grippers opened let go in 0.06 s; the jaw pads press at most 5.8 mm into the bar..

## Verification
`/tests/grade.py` replays `/app/output/trajectory.npz` from the frozen start in two fresh processes (`rcb_rfo.py replay`:
one sim row per control step until the referee ends the attempt). Pass iff the referee's verdict is a success in both
and both end in the same state. The rules: The bar is a competition horizontal bar: 0.028 m thick, 2.8 m above the floor, 2.4 m long between its two uprights, along the y axis; it is fixed and does not turn. Your hands are two-finger grippers (each jaw clamps with up to 528.0 N); they hold the bar by the friction of their jaws only, so the bar can slide along the jaws and out of them. A hand holds the bar while the bar touches both of that gripper's jaws. You start standing on the block and must jump to the bar: the catch is the moment both hands hold the bar after you have left the block (holding it before leaving the block fails). Before the catch you fail if you touch the mats or the floor; you may land back on the block and jump again. After the catch, when for more than 0.05 s a hand has not held the bar you have let go (whether you opened the gripper or the bar slipped out), and there is no regrip; only the grippers may touch the bar; touching the block, the uprights, the mats or the floor fails. Your swing angle is the angle between straight down and the line from the bar to your centre of mass, seen from the side. The giant swing is a full circle of that line after the catch: the angle must travel 360.0 degrees in one direction (forwards or backwards) without turning back; a turn back of more than 5.0 degrees starts the count again from where you turned. The body must be extended over the top: whenever your centre of mass is higher than the bar it must be at least 0.565 m from the midpoint of your two wrists (nine tenths of the dead hang's 0.628 m: a slight pike or arch passes, a tuck or bent arms do not); if it comes closer there, the count starts again from that point. You succeed the moment the count reaches 360.0 degrees. Below the bar you may bend as you like to build up the swing. You fail after 60 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
