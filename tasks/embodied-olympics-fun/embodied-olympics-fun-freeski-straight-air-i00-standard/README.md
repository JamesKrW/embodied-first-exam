## Task
Drop in from the start platform, ride the in-run, take off from the kicker, do a straight air in the air, land on your skis and ride away past the finish line. (Embodied Olympics `freeski-straight-air`; the robot as a service: its head cameras, joint sensors and IMUs, and the venue's fixed camera only.)

## Difficulty
The robot must start itself from a standstill on level snow, ride a 35 degree in-run to about 16 m/s and leave a 25 degree kicker with the right rotation for its trick, finish the rotation in about 1.5 s of air and land on a 36 degree slope with no balance controller of its own. In standard mode the agent has no simulator and no state: it sees through the robot's cameras, acts through the client, and has 5 resets (only the last attempt is graded).

## Reference solution
None (as `embodied-olympics-fun-freeski-straight-air-i00-privileged`).

## Verification
The sim sidecar's referee judges the live attempt, and that is the verdict (`final.json`: `graded_by` live, `replays` 0;
no grading replay, owner 2026-10-17); the frozen start is checked on the live simulator (`instance_mismatch`:
infrastructure), and the video is rendered from the recorded attempt afterwards. `/tests/grade.py` reads
`/data/final.json`: pass iff the live attempt succeeded. The
rules: FIS big air, one jump, judged on one trick: the straight air: no spin (less than half a turn about the vertical) and no flip. Your feet are bound to freestyle skis (1.35 m twin tips). You stand on the level start platform with your boots 0.3 m behind its edge (the start line); the start signal is given at t = 0 and you must leave the platform by your own action (nothing holds or pushes you) within 20.0 s. The course: the platform's edge rounds onto the in-run (38.0 m at 35.0 degrees), a transition and a 5.0 m flat, then the kicker (it rises to its lip at 25.0 degrees, 2.1 m above the flat), a table 15.0 m long at the flat's level, the knoll, the landing (36.0 degrees, 30.0 m long), a transition and the flat outrun. The jump is your flight off the kicker: it starts within 4.0 m of the lip, rising at 10.0 degrees or more. The trick is measured from your pelvis's rotation in the air only (from the moment your last contact with the snow ends until the next contact): the spin about the vertical, counted in half turns of 180 degrees; the flip about the horizontal axis across your take-off direction, after removing the 61.0 degrees a straight air turns forward anyway, counted in full turns of 360 degrees and only if your body went upside down; each to the nearest unit (up to half a unit short or over still counts as that unit). A flip with half a turn of spin is still that flip. You succeed when the jump's rotation is the straight air and the landing is clean: the first contact after the flight is your skis', on the knoll or the landing hill (past the table); from then until both boots cross the finish line (15.0 m past the start of the outrun) no part of you but the boots touches the snow and you are not down for 1.0 s in all (down: on the snow with your pelvis less than 0.4 m above it, measured along its normal, or your trunk more than 70.0 degrees from its normal; the time down shrinks again at a quarter of the rate while you are up); and at the finish line your pelvis is at least 0.4 m above the snow and your skis point along the course (within 30.0 degrees, forwards or backwards). You fail: on any other trick (more or less rotation, a flip where none is asked, a different direction of flip, a take-off riding backwards), on a fall at any time (a part of you other than your boots on the snow, or down for 1.0 s in all), a landing on the table, the body first, a run with no jump, a stop (slower than 0.3 m/s for 3.0 s after the start), no start within 20.0 s, or at 40 s. No oracle; human review in the pull request that adds the benchmark (PROTOCOL §2.7).
